## Church-Turing thesis

> Every effectively computable function is Turing-computable. An effective procedure is a definite mechanical method executable step by step; “computable” in this thesis is not simply “a function exists mathematically.” The slide shorthand is that whatever a computer can do, a TM can do. The standard thesis links effective computation to the formal TM model; broader claims about every physically possible process require care.

Evidence includes the convergence of independently developed formalizations: Turing machines, lambda calculus, general recursive functions, Post-style machines, and idealized stored-program machines all characterize the same class of computable functions. Each is capable of simulating the others in the relevant sense. No demonstrated effective algorithm lies outside the class. Analysis of the elementary operations of a mechanical procedure also supports the identification.

The equivalence of two precisely defined formal models can be a theorem. Identifying those models with the informal pretheoretic notion of all effective procedures is a thesis, because that informal notion is not already a formal mathematical object to which an ordinary proof directly applies. Do not answer “it is a thesis because nobody has bothered to prove it.”

## Universality has limits

SQUARE and PRIME-CHECK are computable examples. HALT-CHECK asks for an algorithm that always decides, for any TM description and input, whether that computation halts. There is no total algorithm that correctly solves every instance of that problem.

A universal machine can simulate a nonhalting computation forever. It does not thereby tell you, in finite time, that the simulated computation will never halt. “Simulate any program” and “decide every property of any program” are different capabilities. A partial universal function is compatible with an uncomputable total halting predicate.

Computability also differs from feasibility. A computable problem may require impractical time or memory. A function that is mathematically uncomputable is not merely slow on today's chips.

## McCarthy's two definitions

> **Intelligence:** “the computational part of the ability to achieve goals in the world.”

> **Artificial intelligence:** “the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs.” [L10, p. 15.]

Both are required for Q23. In this usage, intelligence has kinds and degrees and occurs in humans, some animals, and some machines. It is not defined as having a human body or feeling conscious. Achieving a goal includes selecting useful actions in a world with constraints.

The lecture also divides goal-directed ability into prediction and steering/planning. Prediction anticipates what will happen using a model; steering selects actions leading toward an outcome. Predicting that a road is congested and choosing a different route are connected but distinguishable achievements. An accurate predictor with no capacity to act is not the entire goal-achieving system.

## Symbolic AI and competing approaches

Good Old-Fashioned AI (GOFAI) treats intelligence in terms of structured symbols, rules, search, planning, and knowledge representation. Newell and Simon's physical symbol system hypothesis claims that a physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action. “Necessary” says intelligence requires such organization; “sufficient” says the appropriate organization can produce intelligence. This is a substantive hypothesis, not a consequence of a gate truth table.

Fodor's language-of-thought idea gives symbols an internal structured format. Syntactic transformations are intended to track semantic relations: the system responds to form in a way that preserves relevant relations among represented contents. A mechanism need not consciously inspect meanings at every step for a formal rule to preserve a valid inference.

The course contrasts symbolic, neural-network, dynamical, and Bayesian approaches. They are not all developed equally before this midterm. Know why multiple theoretical approaches fit Boden's description. The existence of successful expert systems, game-playing programs, or visual classifiers does not settle whether a system has general intelligence, subjective experience, or semantic understanding.

## Physical, computational, and mental levels

The physical level includes the mechanism; the computational level includes the abstract operation it implements; the mental question asks whether the resulting organization is sufficient for cognition or understanding. Multiple realizability means an abstract computation can be realized in different materials. It does not mean physical implementation details never matter for whether the computation is correctly performed.
