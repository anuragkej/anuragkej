## Why behaviorism appealed

William James emphasized mental life and introspective observation. Watson wanted psychology to be an objective experimental natural science aiming at prediction and control of behavior. The behaviorist restriction was to publicly observable stimuli and responses, rejecting appeals to private mental entities as scientific explanations.

The gain was experimental discipline: measurable behavior, controlled conditions, and reproducible results. The cost was an overly narrow ban on mechanisms that are not directly observed. Scientific entities can be justified indirectly by the explanatory work they do. Cognitive psychology retains experimental rigor while reopening questions about internal processes.

Do not equate all learning theory with an identical simplistic position. Pavlov, Thorndike, Watson, and Skinner belong to the broad historical background, but the course's criticisms target the exclusion of internal representations and an inadequate stimulus-response account of organized behavior.

## Tolman: learning is not identical to performance

In latent learning, rats explore without reward and later show rapid effective performance when reward becomes available. The reward reveals learning that has already occurred; it was not necessary for all of that learning to happen. Motivation can change the expression of knowledge without being its original source.

In spatial-learning tests, behavior can favor the location of a goal rather than a rigid replay of the same turn sequence. The course's shortcut/radial-path example suggests that rats know where a goal is relative to their surroundings. A cognitive map is an internal representation postulated to explain this flexibility. It is an inference to the best explanation, not a photograph of a map inside the rat's head. [L06, pp. 11-13.]

## Lashley and hierarchical organization

Organized action, including speech, has dependencies extending beyond the immediately preceding movement. A plan can organize components into larger units, and later elements can constrain earlier ones. A flat chain in which each response merely triggers the next struggles to explain this structure. Hierarchy and planning become explanatory alternatives.

## Chomsky: language is structured and productive

A speaker can produce and understand novel sentences, so language cannot be only a memorized list of rewarded strings. Grammar describes rules and structures that generate sentences. Phrase-structure analysis groups words into nested units rather than treating them as an undifferentiated sequence. Transformational accounts relate forms such as active and passive sentences.

Surface similarity can conceal different underlying relations: “John is eager to please” and “John is easy to please” look similar, but John does the pleasing in one interpretation and receives it in the other. Conversely, different surfaces can express closely related underlying content. The examples motivate internal structure and constraints beyond local word association.

The historical deep/surface-structure terminology belongs to the assigned account. Learning the argument does not require claiming every detail of that historical linguistic theory is the final modern theory of language.

## SHRDLU: a working micro-world

Terry Winograd's SHRDLU processes language about a small blocks world, plans actions using a virtual arm, answers questions, and connects language to representations of objects and events. The controlled environment includes objects with properties and spatial relations, allowing the system to interpret requests against a model of the world.

Its interacting components support three broad kinds of work:

- **Syntactic analysis:** relationships among words and sentence structure.
- **Semantic analysis:** relationships between expressions and represented objects/events.
- **Reasoning:** relationships within the modeled world, planning actions, checking constraints, and explaining or remembering operations.

The reading describes a collection of interacting procedures rather than a single magical “understander.” Context can resolve references and ambiguities; when it does not, the system may ask for clarification. A command can require intermediate actions, such as moving an obstructing block before placing another object. This connects language, goals, memory, and action.

SHRDLU's success is real but limited. Its world is small, explicitly represented, and engineered to fit its procedures. Ordinary language requires broad background knowledge, flexible pragmatics, and interaction with an open world. A convincing demonstration in a micro-world does not automatically scale to all human understanding. Contrast ELIZA-style conversational pattern responses with a system that maintains a model used to act on and reason about objects.

## Representation, vehicle, and content

The representational vehicle is the physical or formal item carrying a representation: a voltage pattern, written numeral, or stored symbol. Content is what it represents. The pattern “101” is not intrinsically the number five; that interpretation depends on a coding convention. Computation manipulates vehicles according to their relevant formal properties. Whether and how such manipulation yields meaning will reappear in the Chinese Room.
