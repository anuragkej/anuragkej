## Alan Turing: paragraph-level preparation

Alan Turing was a British mathematician and logician whose 1936 work formalized computation using what are now called Turing machines. It introduced a universal machine and established limits on computation, foundational ideas for computer science. He made major contributions to wartime codebreaking at Bletchley Park, including work related to naval Enigma and the Bombe. His 1950 article proposed the imitation game/Turing Test and explored learning machines. His later work also addressed biological pattern formation/artificial-life-related questions. He was prosecuted for homosexual activity, subjected to hormonal treatment, and died in 1954. The tribute connects these scientific contributions with the injustice of his treatment and later public recognition.

## Tribute details and how they connect to the course

Enigma used electromechanical settings to encode messages. The Bombe helped search possible settings; the film excerpt illustrates restricting a search using expected message material. The important computational ideas are representing a search space, exploiting constraints, and using machinery for systematic search. This was a collective effort; the transcript explicitly warns against crediting the whole operation to Turing alone.

The tribute highlights the 1936 paper's three contributions: TM model, universality/stored-program implications, and uncomputability. The 1950 paper is a different contribution about intelligence and testing. The Turing Award, naming of the machines and computability concepts, the government's apology, later pardons, and the £50 note illustrate posthumous recognition.

## René Descartes: paragraph-level preparation

Descartes was a French philosopher and mathematician whose philosophical project emphasized systematic doubt and the certainty of thinking. Cartesian dualism treats a mind as distinct from a body and as an individual immaterial thinking substance. The human person is a union of thinking and extended substance. The view helps articulate the first-person mind/body contrast and the idea of mental independence, but interaction between the proposed realms creates the problem of mental causation. His importance here is the framework and its explanatory difficulties, not just a biographical date.

## Noam Chomsky: paragraph-level preparation

Chomsky is a linguist associated with formal and generative approaches to language. The assigned history uses his work to show why productive, structured language requires more than memorized stimulus-response chains. Hierarchical phrase structure and differences between surface form and underlying relations helped make internal organization a legitimate explanatory topic. His criticism of behaviorist language accounts belongs to the broader cognitive turn. Know a concrete language example rather than only the label “father of linguistics.”

## Two-sentence preparation for the other six names

**Allen Newell:** A founder of computational cognitive science and AI who asked how mind can occur in the physical universe. With Herbert Simon, he developed symbolic approaches to problem solving and the physical symbol system hypothesis.

**Edward Tolman:** A psychologist whose latent-learning and spatial-navigation studies challenged a simple reinforcement-driven response-chain account. Cognitive maps explain flexible goal-directed behavior that is not just replay of trained turns.

**Terry Winograd:** Created SHRDLU, a program connecting language processing, reasoning, and action in a blocks micro-world. It demonstrates interacting computational components while also exposing limits of restricted-world understanding.

**Michael Tomasello:** Studies human/ape cognition, cooperation, cultural learning, and shared intentionality. His account links early joint attention and collaborative capacities to cumulative culture and distinctively human development.

**Phineas Gage:** Survived a frontal brain injury associated with changes in executive regulation, judgment, and personality despite relative preservation of other abilities. His case supports brain-mind dependence, with historical and recovery qualifications.

**Lev Zasetsky:** Luria's patient suffered posterior brain injury with severe perceptual, spatial, language, and memory difficulties. His preserved purpose and sustained effort contrast with the Gage profile and help illustrate double dissociation.
