## Three theses that must stay separate

| Position | Main claim | What does not follow automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Methodological naturalism | Investigate natural phenomena with scientific methods and natural explanations | The investigator must personally reject religion |
| Ontological naturalism | Reality is natural; there is no supernatural realm | Every worthwhile form of expression must be scientific |
| Scientism, in this course's contrast | Science/scientific idiom exhausts what is worth expressing or knowing | This is identical to every form of naturalism |

Methodological naturalism is about how inquiry proceeds. A religious researcher can follow it by testing natural explanations and not inserting miracles as scientific mechanisms. Ontological naturalism is about what exists. Scientism adds an expansive claim about the reach and authority of scientific expression. One can think everything has a natural basis while still valuing poetry, music, or ordinary moral language as ways of expressing things that a scientific description does not replace. The question bank notes disagreement about how these labels relate; reproduce the course's distinction rather than pretending terminology is universally fixed.

L03 p. 14 particularly emphasizes that natural science requires no specific attitude toward religion and can be practiced by believers, atheists, or agnostics. Include that compatibility claim prominently when reproducing the course's methodological-naturalism definition.

Natural does not mean only untouched wilderness. Human culture, language, technology, and social practices can be part of the natural world. Naturalistic inquiry can study them without reducing every useful explanation to a list of particles.

## Physicalism

Physicalism is the family of views according to which the mental is ultimately physically constituted or determined: there is no independent immaterial mental substance needed to settle mental facts. It is broader than the strict identity claim that every mental category is exactly one brain-state category. The course uses the broad idea that the physical somehow settles the mental while recognizing disagreements about the details.

L04 distinguishes unrestricted physicalism from physicalism specifically about the mental. The latter can leave open the status of abstract entities such as numbers; nominalism denies such abstract entities. The deck also mentions deism, a creator who does not subsequently intervene, to show that some restricted physicalist positions need not settle every religious issue. Keep the exact scope of a thesis separate from stronger claims someone might add to it. Survey percentages in L04 report a historical poll of philosophers, not an argument that majority opinion establishes truth.

Newell's framing asks how the human mind can occur in the physical universe. Computation will offer one possible explanatory bridge. Proving that a circuit computes a function is an engineering accomplishment; establishing that some computation is sufficient for a mind is a further philosophical/scientific issue.

## Phineas Gage

In the assigned account, Gage survived a tamping-iron injury involving frontal/prefrontal regions. Many capacities remained: he could perceive, speak, remember, and move. Yet planning, judgment, emotional/social regulation, reliability, and aspects of personality changed. The important lesson is that competent performance on some familiar abilities can coexist with profound problems organizing life and behavior.

Damasio uses the case against a picture in which rationality and personal identity float free of brain organization. Reasoning in daily life is connected to emotion, valuation, and executive control, not just abstract calculation. A person can possess information yet struggle to use it appropriately to pursue goals.

Historical caution from the discussion sheet: the evidence is limited, later retellings can exaggerate, and recovery/reorganization matters. Avoid “Gage permanently lost all morality” or “his entire personality was destroyed.” Describe the course's comparison in terms of relative impairment and sparing. His case does not prove that one tiny spot houses a complete personality.

## Lev Zasetsky

Zasetsky suffered a wartime injury to left posterior/parieto-occipital regions. Luria documents difficulties integrating the visual world, locating things in space, using language, reading/writing, remembering, and organizing spatial or grammatical relations. His experience could feel fragmented even when individual sensations or fragments of knowledge remained.

Complex relational language is revealing: knowing individual words does not guarantee understanding a sentence whose meaning depends on relations among them. Similarly, seeing an object, naming it, knowing its use, and carrying out a skilled action are distinguishable capacities. The needle/thread examples invite analysis of which component survives; do not collapse all failures into a single “memory problem.” His first-person writing matters because it conveys what damaged processing feels like from inside.

Unlike the broad Gage contrast, Zasetsky retained determination, awareness of his difficulties, sustained purpose, and the drive to reconstruct his life. Writing his journal was both evidence of surviving capacities and a strategy for coping. His impairments were severe; “spared executive function” does not mean every frontal task or every aspect of judgment was perfect.

## Single versus double dissociation

**Single dissociation:** a person performs poorly on task A but relatively well on task B after an injury. This suggests a difference, but perhaps A is simply harder and a general loss of capacity affects it first.

Double dissociation adds a crossed case: person 1 is impaired on A but relatively spared on B; person 2 is impaired on B but relatively spared on A. The reverse pattern makes the simple one-dimensional difficulty explanation less plausible. It supports at least partial functional separability and different supporting mechanisms.

| Course comparison | Executive regulation | Posterior perceptual/language/spatial abilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gage | Relatively impaired | Relatively preserved in the broad comparison |
| Zasetsky | Relatively preserved purpose/control | Relatively impaired |

A double dissociation does not show complete independence, exclude interacting networks, or establish a perfect one-function/one-region map. “Relatively” is doing real work. You need both directions, not two people who fail the same task.

## Inference to the best explanation

This is an explanatory inference: compare accounts of the observations and prefer the one that best explains them. Converging relationships between brain change and mental change favor a physical dependence account. They are not a deductive refutation of every imaginable dualist theory; a dualist can posit that damage interferes with a mind's access or expression, but then must explain the systematic pattern and justify the extra commitments.

Do not reason “brain damage changes behavior, therefore all philosophical questions are solved.” The stronger answer states the evidence, the proposed explanation, competing possibilities, and the limits of the inference.
