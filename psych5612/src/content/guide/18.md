## Reading entry points, without deleting the rest of the assignment

**Flanagan:** all four PDF pages, including references. Boden: start at printed p. 9 for breadth and p. 12 for the definition, then use the surrounding chapter for its scope and theoretical account. Damasio and Luria: read the case narratives and compare impairment with relative sparing; use both discussion sheets for the evidential cautions and comparison prompts.

**Miller & Wood:** all three pages. Ayala: all seven; the culture definition starts on PDF p. 5 and the inheritance explanation continues through p. 7. Tomasello & Herrmann: the six-page excerpt, concentrating on the pattern of physical/social cognition and the proposed social-cultural mechanism.

**Bermúdez Excerpt 1:** §1.1, PDF pp. 2-8 until the next heading; §1.3, pp. 8-11 until §1.4; §2.1, pp. 13-22 until §2.2. Other headings or partial neighboring sections remain in the saved excerpt for context but do not replace these specified targets.

**Harris:** uploaded chapter excerpts, especially pp. 3-17 on abstraction, binary/gates and levels; pp. 19-22 on combinational logic; pp. 24-30 on sequential/synchronous design; pp. 32-33 on timing. The excerpt contains intentional gaps between textbook chapters, not a complete textbook. Patterson & Hennessy: pp. 3-15 logic/composition, 17-18 clocks, 19-21 storage, 22-26 FSMs. Dally & Harting: the entire 12-page excerpt, including state encoding and controller construction. Haugeland: the entire 16-page TM/UTM/VNM excerpt.

**Turing:** all seven sections, not just the imitation-game opening. The local Word file includes an introductory essay; the appendix provides a reflowed copy of Turing's article itself, starting at “1. The Imitation Game.” The original Word file remains available separately if you want the editor's introduction. Bermúdez Chinese Room: pp. 1-6 for the argument/replies, then the grounding discussion and review material. Bray: the 18-page excerpt, with Figs. 3.3 and 3.4 at pp. 10-11 emphasized by the instructor.
